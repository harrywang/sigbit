About
SIGBIT

SIGBIT provides a platform for researchers and practitioners interested in blockchain-driven innovations and technologies, such as Web3.0, DeFi, and NFTs, to exchange ideas and share experiences and knowledge.

Block 001

Mission

04 aims

Blockchain and distributed ledger technologies moved past the proof-of-concept stage some time ago, and the questions they raise are squarely information systems questions: how systems are designed, how incentives are structured, how organizations coordinate without a central intermediary, and how institutions respond.

SIGBIT exists to give that work a community and a set of venues — somewhere the research is read by people who know the difference between a consensus protocol and a marketing claim.

A platform to exchange ideas and share experiences and knowledge.

Block 003

Within AIS

Parent body

SIGBIT is one of the Special Interest Groups of the Association for Information Systems, the professional body for academics specializing in information systems. AIS SIGs are member-run communities organized around a shared research interest: they sponsor conference tracks, run their own events, and give a topic a durable presence in the discipline.

We encourage participants to hold AIS membership, which provides the AIS eLibrary, the association's conferences, and the wider SIG network. Interest in SIGBIT itself is open to everyone.

Block 004

How the SIG is run

Elected

SIGBIT is led by an elected officer team supported by an advisory board of active researchers. Officers organize the SIG's events, coordinate sponsored tracks with conference chairs, and keep the membership informed.

The most useful thing a new member can do is volunteer: review for a sponsored track, propose a webinar speaker, add to the resource list, or help run a workshop.

Block 005

Questions

05 answers
Who can join SIGBIT?
Anyone with a research or professional interest in blockchain and related technologies — faculty, doctoral students, industry researchers, and practitioners. You do not need to have published in the area.
Do I need to be an AIS member?
AIS membership is strongly recommended and is required for some association-side benefits, but joining the SIGBIT mailing list is open to all.
Is there a membership fee?
Any SIG dues are set through AIS. Current fees appear on the join page once confirmed for the year.
How do I propose a workshop or special issue?
Send the topic, organizers, target venue, and timeline to the officer team through the contact page. The SIG can co-sponsor events that serve its membership.
Can practitioners present at SIG events?
Yes. Bridging research and practice is part of the point, and practitioner sessions are a standing part of workshops and the webinar series.
